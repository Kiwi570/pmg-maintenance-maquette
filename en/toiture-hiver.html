Signs you shouldn't ignore.

Too much snow on the roof

  • Doors that are hard to open or close
  • Unusual creaking in the structure
  • A ceiling that sags
  • Cracks on interior walls

An ice dam

  • Large icicles at the roof edge
  • Water dripping from soffits or siding
  • Ice in the gutters
  • Stains or bulging on the ceiling
  • Water coming in

An ice dam should come off as soon as it forms: the bigger it gets, the more water seeps in.

The 24-inch benchmark.

Snow removal is generally recommended when build-up approaches 24 inches, and before rain is forecast after a snowfall. Check where you stand.

What you notice

Our advice

What never goes on your roof

  • Hammers, axes or impact tools
  • Saws or cutting tools
  • Hot water poured on the roof
  • Salt-based products

Also: flat or sloped roofs, balconies, gates, stairs and skylights, plus concrete slabs and formwork on commercial sites. Calcium-based products help prevent an ice dam, not remove one.

Frequently asked questions

The price, in writing.

It depends on roof access, build-up, safety requirements and the time needed. You get a clear written quote before the work, then an invoice and a receipt.

How quickly can you come?

In peak season, often the same day or within 24 to 48 hours, depending on demand and the weather. Texts usually get a faster reply.

How much does snow or ice dam removal cost?

The price depends on roof access, build-up, safety requirements and the time needed. The written quote is free.

Why not clear down to the shingles?

A 1 to 4 inch layer protects the shingles from the shovel. We clear the rest, the vents and the drainage paths.

When should I have my roof cleared?

When build-up approaches 24 inches, or before rain is forecast after a snowfall. Sticking doors, creaking or a sagging ceiling: call right away.

Do salt or hot water work?

No. Hot water and salt-based products should stay off a roof; calcium-based products only help prevent ice dams.
